## User correction and verified facts
The user stated: "It's already passed will be legal 2027." This is **CORRECT**. California **SB 868 (Wiener)**, *Electricity: portable solar generation devices*, was signed by Governor Gavin Newsom **September 30, 2026**, chaptered as **Chapter 985, Statutes of 2026** and (regular-session default, no urgency clause) takes effect **January 1, 2027**. Primary signing announcement: https://www.gov.ca.gov/2026/09/30/governor-newsom-issues-legislative-update-9-30-2026/ . Enrolled statutory text https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/billTextClient.xhtml?bill_id=202520260SB868 ; accessible faithful transcription: https://www.californiabalconysolar.com/sb-868 .

California Public Utilities Code **8530(c)** defines qualifying portable plug-in PV: total aggregated **AC** maximum **1,200 W per dwelling**; designed to connect/disconnect through a receptacle; intended to offset on-site electricity consumption; meet most recent NEC and California Electrical Code; certified **as plug-in PV** by UL or equivalent nationally recognized testing laboratory; independent certified protection that isolates the equipment from the building system to prevent grid backfeed in an outage. **8531** exempts only qualifying devices from utility interconnection requirements/agreements and prior utility approval/extra utility equipment and device-related fees; utility **may require simple online notification** with address and device make/model/size without treating it as an approval application. That exemption expires Jan 1, 2030 unless amended. **8532** establishes a sales prohibition beginning Jan 1, 2030 for receptacle-connected PV that lacks required code, certification and anti-backfeed properties. The text names NO particular UL number; standard identification and acceptance must be verified against actual device certification.

The statute passed already, so the earlier Q90 regulatory proposal is **obsolete as the primary objective**. Replace with implementation readiness: precise current rules and utility notices, certified available equipment and verification of listed components, NEC/California Electrical Code obligations, installation/site/building approvals where applicable, renter/HOA constraints, official rollout and 2030 exemption horizon. Do not conflate enactment with commencement, compliance or permission for an arbitrary unlisted inverter.
